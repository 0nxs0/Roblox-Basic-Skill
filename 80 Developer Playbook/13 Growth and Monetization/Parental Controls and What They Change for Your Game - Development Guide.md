# Parental Controls and What They Change for Your Game — Development Guide

New practical guidance for [[13 Growth and Monetization/Publishing Release/Community Knowledge/Policy - Parental Controls and What They Change for Your Game|Parental Controls and What They Change for Your Game]]. This companion adds a build and validation plan; use the linked source for full reference detail.

## Source-grounded context
Parents of younger players can switch off, limit or approve most of what a game relies on: joining, chatting, spending, trading visibility and play time. Roblox enforces these settings for you, but your game has to degrade gracefully when they are on. A game that assumes chat, unlimited spending or an always-visible inventory will feel broken to a large share of its audience.
**Useful source sections:** Two ways parents manage controls, What parents can change, and what it means for you, Design rules that follow.

## Apply it
Use this topic as part of **improve discovery without misleading players**.
- **Define the contract:** For Parental Controls and What They Change for Your Game, write down behavior, inputs, state owner, visible result, and failure response.
- **Build a first slice:** State the audience promise and first-session value; measure one funnel step before offers.
- **Maintain it:** Separate tuning data from behavior, record assumptions, and provide cleanup or rollback before connecting more systems.
- **Project risk:** Show price and benefits clearly, respect platform rules, and avoid coercive timers.

## Validate before shipping
- **Expected path:** Build one small feature using Parental Controls and What They Change for Your Game and record setup and expected result.
- **Boundary case:** Test missing, invalid, repeated, delayed, or interrupted input where relevant.
- **Integration:** Test with neighboring systems and at target device/player scale.
- **Acceptance check:** Compare conversion with satisfaction and test no-spend progression.
